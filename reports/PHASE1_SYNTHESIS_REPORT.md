## Step 4: Synthesis

### Section A: What we now know, model by model

**Cosmos3-Nano Reasoner.** Wins against baseline on both datasets it has been tried on, but every win comes with a catch the source reports flag themselves. On HATREC (546 of 546 videos, full coverage), it beats the majority-class baseline 6.1 times over on Macro-F1, but 74% of runs hit the 15-second UI watchdog, 2 of 7 classes collapse totally (class 3, "Inflating the valve": 0 of 78 correct, predicted only once across 449 outputs), and confidence is badly miscalibrated (ECE 0.555, the model says "90% sure" and is right 24% of the time). On MMAD, coverage is the real problem: only 622 of 39,670 questions (1.57%) were run, and all 601 parseable ones come from MVTec-AD, the single easiest source per Qwen2-VL's full-dataset breakdown. The two serving backends (the official NVIDIA endpoint vs. a community BNB8-quantized checkpoint on Kaggle) disagree by a lot, 73.16% vs. 49.77% accuracy, with non-overlapping 95% confidence intervals, and the report is explicit that these should never be reported as one number. Matched source-for-source against Qwen2-VL-2B on MVTec-AD only, Cosmos3 does not win (73.16% vs. 75.25%), reversing the naive full-vs-partial comparison that would otherwise look like a Cosmos3 win. On the Anomaly Detection subtask specifically, Cosmos3 loses badly to an "always say defect" baseline (F1 0.25 vs. 0.83). **Unverified:** HATREC shortcut checks (static-frame, temporal-shuffle) were never run despite being flagged as top priority in the report itself.

**Qwen2-VL (2B and 7B variants).** The most rigorously tested model in the repo, and the most consistent. On the full MMAD dataset (39,669 of 39,670 questions, the only full-dataset run in the whole repo), the 2B model clearly beats the majority-letter baseline (64.73% vs. 30.38%, confidence intervals don't overlap), but it loses on the Anomaly Detection subtask alone (F1 0.5837 vs. 0.7564 for "always say defect"). That means the model winning on the headline metric is unfit for exactly the operational task, defect gating, the project cares about. On HATREC, results are much weaker and inconsistent across model size and input mode. The 2B model roughly ties or thin-wins random chance and gets a higher score under a static-frame ablation than with real video (19.23% vs. 17.95%), a shortcut red flag, not a good sign. The 7B model ties on dynamic video and drops below chance on static frames, the opposite direction from the 2B model, unexplained. LLaVA-1.5-7B ties chance almost exactly (14.29% vs. 14.28%). **Unverified:** blind-image control on MMAD was never run (flagged as the single most important missing test in the source PDF), so the 64.73% number cannot yet be attributed to actually looking at the image rather than to answer-format priors or pretraining contamination on this now-public benchmark.

**V-JEPA 2 (ViT-L, linear probe).** Reports a perfect 100% accuracy and Macro-F1 1.0 on HATREC, and its own source PDF opens with a warning banner saying not to use this number as a capability claim. Three independent pieces of evidence in the same report point to a static-appearance shortcut rather than real motion understanding: cosine similarity between test and train clips is 0.9922 (near-duplicate content despite a clean cycle-level split), a 14-frame ablation shows zero degradation from the 64-frame result, and, critically, the underlying notebook (not surfaced in the polished PDF) already ran the exact static-frame control the PDF claims is missing, and it scored 0.9851, barely below the "real video" score of 1.0. This is the strongest concrete evidence of a shortcut anywhere in the repo. **This entire result should be treated as unverified and not usable for any external claim** until re-run on a dataset with real station, operator, and product diversity, per the report's own recommendation.

**XGBoost, LightGBM, Chronos-2 (baseline_specialized group).** Only run on ALPI, and that run used synthetically generated data, not the real ALPI/PIADE Zenodo dataset the project's own `data/README.md` points to. Within that synthetic sandbox, the models tie the naive baseline on state estimation (Macro-F1 around 0.27 across the board, XGBoost, LightGBM, and a fine-tuned Qwen1.5-0.5B are all within 0.005 of the majority-class baseline and of each other) and separate more clearly on next-alarm prediction, where a zero-shot Chronos-T5 (4.60% accuracy) and a fine-tuned Qwen1.5-0.5B (5.80% accuracy) both beat trained XGBoost and LightGBM (4.01%, 3.69%, but that's AUPRC, not accuracy, see Section C). `rule_based`, one of the four models in this baseline group, was never run anywhere in the repo. `majority_class` was only ever run against the synthetic ALPI table and the 12-row `dummy` smoke-test fixture, never against a real dataset.

**LLM text-only (`llm_text_only` group).** The model registry (`configs/models.yaml`) specifies this group should test "language ability alone, no fine-tune" with a strong text LLM, `needs_training: false`. What was actually run is a fine-tuned Qwen1.5-0.5B (500M params, explicitly trained on the target task) on synthetic ALPI text. This doesn't answer the research question the group was designed to answer, and the model size (500M) doesn't match "strong text-only LLM" either.

**GPT-4o.** Registered in `configs/models.yaml` and has a runner stub (`evaluation/examples/commercial_api/gpt4o.py`), but no result exists anywhere in the repo. `Phase1_Report_Final.md`'s title implies both GPT-4o and Gemini were tested. Only Gemini numbers appear in the body.

**Gemini (3.5 Flash and Flash-Lite).** Tested on ALPI (B5) and MMAD (B3), zero-shot. Both clearly beat their stated baselines (ALPI: 46.6 to 57.8% Macro-F1 vs. 0.93% persistence baseline; MMAD: 45.8 to 56.9% accuracy, no baseline given in that file). The full-size Flash nominally outperforms Flash-Lite on both datasets, but confidence intervals overlap in every case, not a statistically significant difference, and the report says so explicitly, which is a rare and welcome case of a report not overclaiming. Sample size for Flash is hard-capped at 53 (ALPI) and 72 (MMAD) by free-tier API quota, not by design. An earlier MMAD run (n=100, n=250) had a genuine data leak (the file path leaked the label) and was caught and fixed before the numbers reported here, a good example of the process working, documented in Section 3 of that report.

### Section B: What we now know, dataset by dataset

**HATREC** (video, 546 clips, 7-class assembly-action classification, mapped to B1). Tests whether a model can tell apart 7 short, visually similar hand-assembly actions from video. Known weakness, confirmed multiple times in this repo: the dataset does not force models to use motion. V-JEPA2's static-frame ablation (0.9851 vs. 1.0 "real video") and Qwen2-VL-2B's static-frame result beating its dynamic-video result (19.23% vs. 17.95%) both point the same direction: appearance (workbench layout, which parts are present, hand position) carries most of the signal, motion carries little. `compat_matrix.yaml` already flags this dataset as having "known issues from prior runs: shortcut solvable via static color/appearance, and train/test leak detected for V-JEPA 2." Models run here: V-JEPA2 (trained probe, flagged unreliable), Cosmos3-Nano (zero-shot, wins baseline but operationally unusable per its own report), Qwen2-VL 2B/7B and LLaVA-1.5-7B (zero-shot, mostly at or barely above chance).

**MMAD** (static industrial defect images, ICLR 2025 external benchmark, 8,366 images and 39,672 MCQs, mapped to B3). Tests defect detection, classification, localization, and description on real manufacturing images across 4 sub-sources (MVTec-AD, GoodsAD, VisA, MVTec-LOCO) that vary 20 points in difficulty (55.2% to 75.3% for Qwen2-VL). Known weakness: the Anomaly Detection subtask specifically is class-imbalanced (about 61% "has defect") and every model tested so far (Qwen2-VL-2B, Cosmos3-Nano) loses to a trivial "always say defect" baseline on F1. The accuracy numbers that look like wins are being carried by the easier subtasks (Object Classification, Object Analysis), not by the operationally important one. Only Qwen2-VL-2B has been run on the full dataset. Cosmos3-Nano's MMAD result covers 1.57% of it, entirely from the easiest sub-source, and is explicitly marked non-comparable to any full-dataset number. Gemini's runs (n=72 to 456) are clean but small and have no baseline recorded in their own report.

**ALPI** (alarm and event logs from packaging machinery, tabular and event data, mapped to B0/B5 in the compat matrix but run as "B1"/"B5" in practice). Tests state estimation and next-alarm prediction from a 60-second sliding window of alarm events. Biggest known weakness: the XGBoost, LightGBM, Chronos, and Qwen1.5 results were computed entirely on a synthetically generated stand-in table, not on real ALPI/PIADE data. The notebook itself prints that it's auto-generating the raw table because it couldn't find a real file — even though the real 429,394-row file loads correctly one cell earlier and is then silently discarded. **This has been fixed in the notebook as of 2026-08-04 (the auto-generate fallback is gone), but no re-run against real data has happened yet, so this is still the biggest known weakness of the current numbers.** The Gemini results (via `Phase1_Report_Final.md`) appear to use real ALPI data and are the only trustworthy ALPI numbers in the repo, though their B5 split uses a random shuffle rather than a time-respecting split, a weaker leak defense for a next-event-prediction task than the time-ordered 80/20 split the (synthetic) XGBoost notebook used. B0 (schema and tag understanding), despite being in the compat matrix's ALPI allow-list, has zero results anywhere.

**MIMII** (industrial audio, 4 machine types: fan, pump, slider, valve, mapped to B3). Tests audio anomaly detection and, because this is the one place in the repo that actually ran the "representation experiment" the project keeps recommending elsewhere, compares 1D audio-feature vectors against 2D Mel-spectrogram images as input representations. The image representation (Spectrogram+ResNet18, 0.9866 mean AUC-ROC, including a suspicious perfect 1.0000 on SLIDER) beats the vector representation (Whisper+XGBoost, 0.9316) for trained models, while a zero-shot VLM (Qwen2-VL) reading the same spectrogram images scores at chance (0.4960), confirming that visual pattern recognition on a spectrogram is not something a general-purpose VLM does for free without training. No leak or shortcut check is mentioned anywhere in this report, and the report itself has no backing notebook or raw predictions file committed to this repo, so none of its numbers can be independently re-derived here.

**Other configured sources with zero results:** TEP, Assembly101, and IndustReal all have `compat_matrix.yaml` entries (several marked `inferred: true`, meaning even the allow-list itself is a reconstruction from a garbled planning doc) but no report, notebook, or result file anywhere in the repo.

### Section C: Cross-cutting findings worth keeping

**Accuracy is the wrong headline metric on every imbalanced task in this suite, and it fooled at least one report.** `ALPI_BASELINE_BENCHMARK_REPORT.md`'s Task B5 table stacks four models' scores into one "Kết quả" column and calls Qwen1.5-0.5B "the peak, +44.6% over XGBoost," but XGBoost and LightGBM's numbers in that column are AUPRC (0.0401, 0.0369) while Chronos-T5 and Qwen1.5's numbers in the same column are exact-match accuracy (0.0460, 0.0580). These are not the same metric, and the "+44.6%" headline compares apples to oranges. On MMAD's Anomaly Detection subtask, both Qwen2-VL-2B and Cosmos3-Nano look fine on accuracy but lose to a trivial constant-answer baseline on F1. The metric choice alone flips the win/loss verdict. Any future leaderboard for this benchmark needs one metric per task, enforced, not whatever the model happened to print.

**Architecture relationship: the Cosmos3 community checkpoint is loaded as Qwen3-VL.** `evaluation/examples/cosmos3_nano/02_cosmos3_nano_mmad.pdf` notes the BNB8-quantized Kaggle checkpoint is loaded via `Qwen3VLForConditionalGeneration` because "the Cosmos checkpoint declares a Qwen3-VL-compatible architecture." The weights are still Cosmos's, not swapped for Qwen's, but this is a real architectural dependency worth recording, and it may partly explain why this backend's outputs diverge so much from the official NVIDIA endpoint (73.16% vs. 49.77% on the identical MVTec-AD subset).

**Static-frame and motion-shortcut checks keep finding the same problem, dataset-wide.** Every model tested on HATREC that got a static-frame or single-frame ablation (V-JEPA2, CNN+XGBoost, Qwen2-VL-2B) showed little to no degradation from removing real motion. In one case (Qwen2-VL-2B) static frames scored higher. This isn't a per-model finding, it's a dataset finding: HATREC as currently sampled does not require temporal reasoning to solve at the reported accuracy levels, and any future HATREC number needs a static-frame control run alongside it before being trusted, per `compat_matrix.yaml`'s own existing warning.

**Sampling matters, and coverage bias is a form of shortcut too.** Cosmos3's 622-question MMAD sample being 100% MVTec-AD (the easiest of 4 sub-sources, about 20 points easier than the hardest) inflated its apparent standing until the report itself did the source-matched correction. This is a distinct failure mode from data leakage or a modeling shortcut, it's a sampling problem, and it produced the same effect (an optimistic, unusable number) that leak checks are designed to catch. Stratified, source-matched sampling should be mandatory before any cross-model comparison on MMAD.

**Self-hosted and free-tier infrastructure is a real constraint on sample size, not just a footnote.** Gemini's sample sizes (n=53 to 72 for the full "Flash" model) were hard-capped by Google AI Studio free-tier quota (429 RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED errors), not by experimental design, and that's exactly why its confidence intervals are wide enough to overlap Flash-Lite's on every comparison. Cosmos3's NVIDIA-endpoint HATREC run hit a 15-second UI watchdog on 74% of samples, degrading measured accuracy independent of model capability. Both point the same direction: comparing "capability" across models run on different infrastructure or quota tiers silently compares infrastructure as much as it compares models. Any leaderboard needs to record run infrastructure and note where sample size is quota-limited vs. designed.

**When a report catches its own mistake, it's worth noticing, and this happened twice, well.** `evaluation/examples/cosmos3_nano/02_cosmos3_nano_mmad.pdf` explicitly walks back a naive 73.16%-vs-64.73% comparison that would have made Cosmos3 look better than Qwen2-VL, once it re-checked against a source-matched subset. `evaluation/examples/commercial_api/Phase1_Report_Final.md` documents catching and fixing a genuine path-based data leak on MMAD (accuracy dropped from about 88% to about 46% after the fix) before publishing. These are the two strongest examples of the process working as intended in the whole repo, and worth using as the internal template for future reports. Most of the other reports (MIMII, the ALPI baseline notebook) don't show this kind of self-check.

### Section D: Gaps against the Phase 1 gate conditions

**1. Benchmark task/schema/metric frozen as v0.1: about 35%.** `configs/tasks.yaml` defines all of B0 through B10 with a primary metric each, which is real progress, but B6's secondary metric is explicitly marked as a best-effort reconstruction from garbled source text, 6 of 11 tasks (B2, B4, B6, B7, B8, B9) have no data source at all, and, more importantly, the task IDs actually used in real runs don't reliably match the frozen schema (the ALPI "B1" results use a task ID `compat_matrix.yaml` doesn't even allow for that dataset). A schema that the actual experiments don't follow isn't frozen in any operational sense yet.

**2. At least 3 data sources and 5 baseline families run end to end: about 30%.** Real, non-synthetic data sources with an actual result: HATREC, MMAD, MIMII, exactly 3, which nominally clears the bar, but ALPI's results are synthetic and TEP, Assembly101, IndustReal have zero runs, so it's 3 out of 7 configured sources, and one of the "working" 3 (MIMII) has no reproducible artifact in-repo. Baseline families: of the 5 groups in `configs/models.yaml`, only `mllm_zero_shot` (Cosmos3, Qwen2-VL) is cleanly represented across multiple datasets. `embedding_probe` (V-JEPA2) ran but is self-flagged unreliable. `baseline_specialized` is missing `rule_based` entirely and only has `majority_class` against a synthetic or dummy target. `llm_text_only` ran the wrong kind of model (fine-tuned 500M, not the zero-shot "strong text LLM" the spec calls for). `commercial_api` is half-done (no GPT-4o anywhere). And critically, none of this ran through the actual `run_eval.py` and `compat_matrix`-validated pipeline. Every real number came from ad hoc notebooks that don't touch `src/worldbench/scoring/score_run.py` at all.

**3. Representation experiment (text/plot/embedding) completed: about 45%.** MIMII is a genuine, completed instance of exactly this experiment (audio-vector vs. spectrogram-image representation, with a trained and a zero-shot condition each), that's real, useful evidence. But it's the only one. The ALPI report explicitly recommends the equivalent chart or plot representation experiment for its own dataset as future work and never runs it, and no representation experiment exists for HATREC or MMAD (both are already single-modality, video and static image respectively, so a text/embedding comparison hasn't been attempted there either).

**4. Failure cases properly categorized, not just averaged: about 65%.** This is the strongest gate condition in the current work. `02_cosmos3_nano_mmad.pdf`, `03_cosmos3_nano_hatrec.pdf`, `01_qwen2vl_2b_mmad.pdf`, and `Phase1_Report_Final.md` all include specific labeled error examples with sample IDs, model reasoning excerpts, predicted-vs-true labels, and named failure categories (hallucination, label confusion, spatial-grounding failure, majority-class bias, format error), several with Pareto-style error-distribution tables. This practice is not universal, `MIMII_BENCHMARK_REPORT.md` and the ALPI baseline notebook have no error taxonomy at all, but where it happened, it happened well.

**5. Real-data acquisition spec written: about 50%.** `data/README.md` exists and lists all 7 sources with download locations and license notes, which is the spec itself, done reasonably well. But it is not being followed in at least one confirmed case (ALPI ran on synthetic data despite the spec pointing to a real Zenodo record), and there's no verification step anywhere confirming the other "real" runs (HATREC, MMAD, MIMII) actually pulled from the locations the spec names rather than some other local copy. A written spec that isn't checked against actual runs only half-counts.

### Section E: Recommended next steps, in priority order

1. **Re-run the ALPI baseline notebook (`notebooks/ALPI_baseline_models.ipynb`) against real, downloaded ALPI/PIADE data** (per `data/README.md`, https://zenodo.org/records/7071747), and republish `reports/ALPI_BASELINE_BENCHMARK_REPORT.md` from that run. The synthetic-data fallback that caused this problem has been removed from the notebook (2026-08-04), so a re-run will now actually use real data instead of silently repeating the same bug — but the re-run itself still hasn't happened. This remains the single highest-priority fix. Every ALPI number currently in the repo is synthetic, and Section D's gate-2 estimate goes up materially once this is real. **Flag for double-check before any external claim: literally every number in the current ALPI report.**
2. **Fix the ALPI B5 metric-mixing bug and re-derive the "+44.6%" claim.** Recompute AUPRC for Chronos-T5 and Qwen1.5, and accuracy for XGBoost and LightGBM (or pick one metric for all four) before repeating any comparison between them. This is cheap, no new inference is needed, just re-scoring existing predictions, and it directly fixes a claim in the current report that its own numbers don't support.
3. **Run the static-frame and temporal-shuffle shortcut controls that are already flagged as missing, starting with Cosmos3-HATREC.** V-JEPA2's own committed notebook already shows what this control looks like and how decisive it is (0.9851 static vs. 1.0 real, the story is basically already told for that model). Cosmos3-Nano's HATREC report calls this its top missing test. It's a cheap, already-scoped follow-up. Same for MMAD's blind-image control (flagged as top priority in both the Cosmos3 and Qwen2-VL MMAD PDFs): until it's run, the 64.73% and 73.16% headline numbers cannot be attributed to actually looking at the image.
4. **Run GPT-4o.** It's the one commercial_api model with zero results despite a working stub script, and the project's own `configs/models.yaml` frames the commercial_api group's research question specifically around comparing to "MMAD's published GPT-4o baseline (~74.9%)," which is currently impossible to do with data in this repo.
5. **Run `rule_based` on at least one real dataset.** It's one of four models in the `baseline_specialized` group and has never been run anywhere. Cheap to do (per `configs/models.yaml`, `needs_training: false`), and needed to establish the actual "floor" the group's research question is asking about.
6. **Correct or re-scope the `llm_text_only` group.** Either fine-tune a genuinely "strong" text-only LLM as the spec calls for, or update `configs/models.yaml` to reflect that a small fine-tuned model is the intended design. Right now the config and the actual run disagree, and the research question ("is language ability alone sufficient, zero-shot") hasn't actually been tested by anyone yet.
7. **Route at least one future run through the real pipeline** (`scripts/run_eval.py` calling `src/worldbench/scoring/score_run.py`, validated against `compat_matrix.yaml`, writing to `results/{model}__{dataset}__{task}.jsonl`). Every real result to date bypasses this entirely. The only file that follows the intended convention is the 12-row dummy smoke test. Until something real goes through it, it's untested machinery, not infrastructure the team can rely on.
8. **Widen Gemini's sample size past the free-tier quota wall** (currently n=53 to 72) so the Flash-vs-Flash-Lite comparison has a chance of resolving instead of permanently overlapping in confidence interval. Either paid-tier API access or key rotation, both already suggested in `Phase1_Report_Final.md`.
9. **Before repeating externally:** flag the V-JEPA2 HATREC 100% result, the Spectrogram+ResNet18 MIMII 1.0000-on-SLIDER result, and the Cosmos3 combined-backend 622-question MMAD number as unverified and do-not-cite. The first two need the controls in item 3. The third is explicitly disclaimed by its own source report as an invalid merge.
